# Vision

`fly-with-me` exists so that a person can leave a web page open beside their work, stay focused on that work, and feel calmer, and now and then a small jolt of awe, each time they glance at it.
It serves anyone with a browser and a link, and it turns a seed into an endless, plausible world crossed by a bird.
It owns exactly one thing: the flight, and the experience of watching it.

## The experience

A person leaves the page open beside their work and stays focused on the work; when they glance over, within a few seconds they are calmer than before, and now and then they feel a small jolt of awe.
It is never meant to be their focus, and it never asks to become it: no goal, no score, no failure, no notification, and no reason to keep looking except that it is beautiful and it keeps going.
Most of the time it is do nothing and watch; the actions the page invites are actively kept to the fewest possible.
They ride a bird, because a bird is the one point of view that crosses forests, deserts, coasts and mountains, rises above the clouds and drops back under them, without the scene ever explaining itself.
The flight is slow, continuous and quiet; most of the time nothing happens, on purpose, and a rare moment lands because it is rare.
The flight is mostly alone, and meant to feel so; now and then company comes, a flock for a while, one day perhaps another person's bird in the same sky, and the companionship is pleasant because it is occasional and asks nothing of either of them, not even a wave.
The world is never the same twice and always makes sense: coasts look like coasts, forests thin toward the snow line, and the ruins of an older world are found, never announced.
Whatever brings the reality and diversity of real nature into the world is welcome, weather included.
A day passes in minutes, not hours, with dawn and dusk given their time and a dark night full of stars, so one sitting sees all of it; the world's diversity is there to be experienced, not waited for.
The bird turns of its own accord to meet the low sun, the moon and the core of the Milky Way.
A hand on the bird takes its course or its height and gives it back; the flight carries on from where it was left, and the page remembers.

## A link is enough

The experience is as easy to reach as a link and as easy to keep as a page: no account, no install, nothing to run.
Today the page is one file that makes every world, light, cloud, tree, ruin and sound from a seed on the viewer's machine and fetches only Three.js; that is how it is built, not what it is.
Assets and other files are welcome when the experience is better for them, a hand-painted leaf in a library file included; the file count and the fetch count are technical details and never the test.
Anything that lets the page open in more places, offline and from a copied file included, is welcome, even at the price of carrying a dependency.
The art is original: a photograph or a game frame may inform it, and none is copied into it.
The same seed opens the same world for everyone, a link carries it, and the page remembers a flight without being asked.

## Left on for hours

The page runs for hours beside the work, so what it costs the machine is part of the experience: it must not drain a battery or burn a GPU.
Cost is measured at held vantages of one seed's world, as frame time and as the share of each second the GPU and the main thread stay busy, and a change shows what it did to that cost.
A change that makes the page cheaper and leaves the picture alone is welcome; a change that costs the machine more must earn it in the picture.
A quieter frame rate on battery, or one quiet switch for it, is welcome, and it is not a second look.
A phone that cannot hold the approved look smoothly may skip what it cannot afford, the reconstruction and the bloom, and keep the same flight; that is a budget, not a second look.
No frame rate is promised anywhere.

## One look, judged by a human eye

Looks are explored side by side, one is approved on a review board, and the rest leave the page, kept only in history and as fixed reference studies.
No look switch or alternative rendering mode ships; the approved reference is what a change is compared against, and the laptop's picture is that reference.
Visual quality is ensured by human review in a real browser: a change to the approved rendering, water or sky shows its before and after at the same seed and vantage, read from the finished picture, and is judged by eye before it is judged by numbers.
Rarity stays rare; a ruin, a low pass, a night turn or a title card is never made louder or more frequent so that it is seen more.
The viewer's hand outranks the flight's plan, and the camera sits rigid on the bird and never recenters itself.

## The engine serves the experience, and the library makes it easy to extend

Places and things enter through `library/` as data, as small functions over kits, or as a painted picture, validated at load and refused by name when they break the contract, so a contributor never has to touch light, sky, fog, shadows or shaders to add to the world.
That is the easy path, not the only one: a change to the engine is welcome when it adds to the calm or the awe, light shafts through the cloud deck at dawn for one, and it carries the engine's rules and a before-and-after with it.
When a contribution needs a capability the library lacks, adding it to a kit with a validator rule is preferred over a one-off in the engine, so the next contributor gets it too.
What stays closed is the world's coherence, not the engine: one climate rule, one swatch envelope, one masonry and one soft light over all of it, so ten biomes and five kinds of ruin read as one world.

## Scope

It is not a game, a Three.js showcase, a media player, a framework or a component library.
It is not the viewer's focus: every control added makes it likelier that the calm breaks, and a key that summons a ruin or a coast is a goal, and there are none.
A choice that does exist, a bird or a quiet switch, lives out of sight behind the corner, so the picture and the controls line stay as they are and a viewer who never looks never knows.
It does not count, track or report its viewers; knowing how many people fly does not contribute to the experience.
A phone deserves the same experience as a laptop, not more controls.
It is not a home for a second look or a second engine; an exploration is kept in history, not on the page.
More than one bird may fly here one day, but a choice is never forced on the viewer before the flight begins.
Other people in the same sky are wanted one day, as distant company; a multiplayer element is judged like everything else by the experience it gives.

A change aligns when it makes the viewer calmer or gives them a moment of awe, helps them stay focused on their work rather than on the page, brings more of real nature's reality and diversity into the world, keeps it plausible, endless and coherent, and shows in a real browser what it did to the approved look and to the machine, whichever door it comes through.
A change should be resisted when it invites an action or adds a control to the picture, when it asks the viewer to choose or to attend, when it adds a second look to the published page, when it makes a rare thing common or a quiet thing loud, when it slows the day so that a sitting sees less of the world, when it costs the machine more for hours without the picture earning it, or when it counts or reports on viewers.
